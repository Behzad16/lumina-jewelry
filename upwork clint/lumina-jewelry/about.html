Nine people, one bench, no shortcuts

Lumina began in 2016 with a rented bench, a second-hand rolling mill and a stubborn idea: that fine jewelry should be something you actually wear.

How it started

A workshop, not a warehouse

Our founder trained as a goldsmith in Florence, then spent six years designing for a heritage house where she watched beautiful pieces sit in safes, worn twice a year.

Lumina was the answer to that. Pieces substantial enough to feel like fine jewelry, but comfortable enough to sleep in — solid gold instead of plate, rounded edges instead of sharp settings, clasps that actually hold.

Ten years on we are still on Greene Street, still nine people, and still making everything in the room where it is designed. We have turned down wholesale offers three times to keep it that way.

What we stand on

Three promises we don't bend

01

Solid metal, always

We work in solid 18k gold and gold vermeil over sterling silver — never plated brass. Vermeil is always at least 2.5 microns, ten times the industry minimum, which is why our pieces do not turn.

02

Traceable stones

Every diamond is either lab-grown in the United States or natural and traceable to a named mine under the Kimberley Process. We publish the origin of each stone on the certificate that ships with your piece.

03

Made to be repaired

Nothing is glued and nothing is unserviceable. Every Lumina piece carries a lifetime warranty, and we will re-tip prongs, re-polish and re-string for free, for as long as you own it.

The atelier

How a piece is made

Step 01

Drawn by hand

Every design starts as a pencil drawing, then a wax model we can put on a finger before a single gram of gold is cut.

Step 02

Cast in recycled gold

We cast in small batches from 100% recycled 18k gold, refined thirty blocks away in the Diamond District.

Step 03

Set by one person

One setter takes a piece from raw casting to finished stone. It is slower, and it is why the settings sit level.

Step 04

Checked, boxed, signed

Each piece is inspected under a loupe, hand-polished, and packed in a box tied by the person who finished it.
